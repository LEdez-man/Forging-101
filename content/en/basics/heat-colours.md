In hand forging you measure temperature by eye. The **heat colour** shows the steel's temperature from about 500 °C. **Temper colours** are oxide colours on bright steel between 200 and 350 °C and show the state during [[en/advanced/heat-treatment|tempering]].

![[assets/diagrams/gluehfarben-en.svg|Heat colours and temper colours]]

## Forging ranges (guide values)
| Material | Start | Finish | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Mild steel S235 / A36 | ~1150–1250 °C | ~800 °C | Forgiving, barely hardenable |
| C45 / 1045 | ~1100 °C | ~850 °C | Hardenable, for medium-hard tools |
| 51CrV4 / 6150 (spring steel) | ~1100 °C | ~850 °C | Tools, punches, chisels |
| O1 / O2 tool steel | ~1050 °C | ~850 °C | Check the data sheet, don't overheat |
| Wrought iron | up to welding heat | ~800 °C | Fibrous; welds very well |

These are guide values. The manufacturer's **data sheet** (or *Machinery's Handbook* / *Tabellenbuch Metall*) is authoritative.

## Rules of thumb
- **"Forge while it glows" is too crude.** Stop heavy forming below dark red (~750 °C).
- **Blue brittleness:** between about 200 and 350 °C steel is more brittle than at room temperature. Never strike or straighten in this range.
- **Magnet test:** above the Curie temperature (≈ 768 °C) steel is non-magnetic. For carbon steels around 0.8 % C this is a usable hardening indicator (harden about 30–50 °C above it).
- **Light:** judge colours in a dim shop. In direct sunlight steel looks 100 °C or more "too cold", so you will overheat it.
- **Burning:** sparks from the work in the fire (other than at welding heat) mean the steel is burning. The structure cannot be saved and must be cut off.
