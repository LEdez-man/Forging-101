Control comes before force. A well-guided 1 kg hammer moves more material than a cramped 2 kg swing.

![[assets/diagrams/schlaege-en.svg|Basic blows]]

## Stance & grip
- **Stance:** feet shoulder-width apart and slightly staggered, body close to the anvil. Your hammer hand is over the centre of the anvil.
- **Grip:** **loose**, thumb along the side of the handle (not on top), held in the rear third. A tight grip tires you and leads to tendonitis and tennis elbow.
- **Motion:** shoulder, elbow and wrist work together. Let the hammer **rebound** and carry that energy into the next blow.
- **Rhythm:** even beats. The light taps on the anvil between working blows that you often see keep the rhythm; they do no forming.

## The basic blows
| Blow | How | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| **Flat blow** | Hammer face parallel to the anvil face, work centred under the hammer | Even drawing / planishing |
| **Angled blow** | Hammer deliberately tilted | Pushes material one way (tapers, bevels, correcting a rhombus) |
| **Half-faced blow** | Half the face over the anvil edge | Step / shoulder (→ [[en/techniques/shouldering\|Shouldering]]) |
| **Edge blow** | With the edge of the face | Groove, marking (carefully: risk of cold shuts) |
| **Peen blow** | With the cross-peen | Directional flow (→ [[en/techniques/spreading\|Spreading]] / [[en/techniques/drawing-out\|Drawing out]]) |
| **Leverage blow** | Work laid over the near or far edge, blow beside the support | Bending, offsets |

Christ Centered Ironworks describes five basic blows (*5 Basic Hammer Blows*). Black Bear Forge has a whole video on the **half-faced blow**.

## Exercise
**Control drill:** strike a cold lead bar or a lump of clay and check the marks. They should be equally deep, with no half-moons. Then hot: draw 12 mm square to exactly 8 mm *without* a rhombus.
