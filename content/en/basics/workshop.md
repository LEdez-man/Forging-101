## Forge
| Type | Advantages | Disadvantages |
|---|---|---|
| **Coal forge** (bituminous smithing coal, coke) | Localized heat, easy forge welding, traditional | Smoke, clinker, fire management has to be learned |
| **Gas forge** (propane) | Clean, ready at once, even heat | Whole chamber hot (little local heat), CO, welding heat only with a good forge |
| **Induction** | Very precise, fast, no fuel | Expensive, coils per section |

**Fire management (coal):** build a compact, deep heart of fire over the tuyere. The work lies *in* the fire, not on top of it. Wet fresh coal at the edge so it cokes there. Clear clinker from the tuyere regularly. An oxidizing (air-rich) fire scales heavily; welding needs a **reducing**, deep fire.

## Anvil
- **Weight:** at least 50 kg (110 lb) for hand work, better 75–150 kg. Rule of thumb: the anvil should weigh at least about 50 times the hammer.
- **Height:** face at **knuckle to wrist height** with the arm hanging relaxed (Mark Aspery, *Anvil height and hammer choice*). Too high costs strength, too low causes tilted blows and back pain.
- **Patterns:** London pattern (with step/table), German/South German pattern (horn + square horn), double-horn anvils. All have a **hardened face**, **hardy hole** (square, for tools) and **pritchel hole** (round, for punching).
- **Edges:** sections with different radii (sharp → round) are useful, see [[en/techniques/shouldering|Shouldering]].
- **Stand:** hardwood stump or steel stand, anvil bolted or chained down (against walking and ringing).

## Hammers
- **Hand hammer** 1–1.5 kg (2–3 lb; beginners nearer 1 kg), cross-peen or engineer's hammer; face slightly crowned, **edges radiused**
- **Sledge** 3–5 kg (with a striker)
- Special hammers: set hammer, flatter, hot cut, fuller (handled)

## Tongs
Tongs must grip the work **along two lines**. A point or edge grip twists out. Basic set: **flat-jaw**, **V-bit** (square, round, octagon), **wolf-jaw/pickup**, **box-jaw** for flat bar. A ring (*rein clip*) holds the reins. Making your own tongs is the classic first tool project → [[en/advanced/toolmaking|Toolmaking]].

## Other equipment
Leg vise, hardy, bolster/swage block, slack tub, wire brush, chalk/soapstone, calipers, dividers, square.
