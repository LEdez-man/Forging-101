In forging the **volume stays constant**. The only losses are scale (about 1–3 % per heat) and offcuts. This gives the smith's most important calculation:

> **new length = old length × old section ÷ new section**

## Examples
| Task | Calculation | Result |
|---|---|---|
| 20 mm square, 100 mm long → 10 mm square | 100 × 400 ÷ 100 | 400 mm |
| Draw 16 mm round to 8 mm round, target 300 mm | 300 × (8² ÷ 16²) | **75 mm** stock (+ allowance) |
| Square point 12 mm, 90 mm long (pyramid) | V = ⅓ · 12² · 90 = 4320 mm³ → 4320 ÷ 144 | **30 mm** stock |
| Ring 50 mm ID from 10 mm round | π · (50 + 10) | **≈ 189 mm** |

**Allowances:** about 5–10 % for scale and finishing, much more for forge welds (upset allowance).

## Practice: rules & gauges
- **Hook rule**: a rule with a hook at the end, so you can measure hot steel without touching it ([Black Bear Forge – Making and using a hook rule](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=hXn288rM3PM))
- **Chalk / soapstone** for marks, centre punch for permanent ones
- **Templates** (1:1 drawing on the bench) for scrolls and grilles
