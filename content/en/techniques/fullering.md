**Fullering** means forging a groove with a rounded tool (the fuller). It isolates zones, e.g. for balls, leaf stems or collars, and it moves material quickly in one direction, so it draws out more efficiently than the face.

![[assets/diagrams/kehlen-en.svg|Fullering with top and bottom fuller]]

## Tools
- **Top fuller** (handled) and **bottom fuller** in the hardy hole
- **Spring fuller**: both halves joined by a spring, easy to use without a striker
- **Fullering jig**, **guillotine tool**
- The anvil **horn** and the **cross-peen** act as improvised fullers

## Steps
1. Mark the position. For a two-sided groove, fit the bottom fuller.
2. Bring the bar to **yellow heat**, lay it over the bottom fuller with the top fuller exactly above it, and strike. Turn 180° (or 90°) every 2–3 blows to keep the groove centred.
3. When fullering repeatedly to draw out, **planish** the ripples afterwards with the face or a flatter.

> [!tip] Fuller profile
> Fullers have round or elliptical profiles (*teardrop fuller*), never sharp edges. A sharp groove acts as a notch and causes cold shuts or cracks.
