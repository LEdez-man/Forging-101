At the forge you don't drill a hole, you **punch** it (material is driven out as a slug) or **slit** it (material is only parted and displaced). Then you open it to final size and shape with a **drift**. Uses: pass-throughs (grilles), rivet holes, hammer and axe eyes, tong pivots, bottle openers.

![[assets/diagrams/lochen-en.svg|Punching – from both sides, drive the slug out, drift]]

## Punch or slit?
| | Punching | Slit & drift |
|---|---|---|
| Material | removed as a slug | kept, displaced sideways |
| Section around the hole | weakened | bar swells into an eye, hardly weakened |
| Typical | rivet holes, small round holes | pass-throughs, hammer/axe eyes, large holes |
| Rule of thumb | hole diameter ≤ about ⅓ of bar width | slit length ≈ **½ circumference** of the finished hole (round hole: π·d/2) |

## Tools
- **Punch**: tapered, flat end slightly smaller than the hole, made of tool steel (e.g. 1045, S1-type)
- **Slitter**: narrow, blunter than a hot cut, rounded edge
- **Drift**: tapered body with the final section (round, oval, square, eye shape)
- **Bolster** (plate with holes), pritchel hole or hardy hole to drive the slug out
- Coal dust or graphite as release agent, water for cooling

## Steps (punching)
1. Centre-punch the hole position. Bring the bar to **yellow to white-yellow heat** (hotter than for drawing out).
2. Set the punch, check it is upright, and drive it in hard. Drop a pinch of coal dust into the hole so the punch doesn't stick.
3. **Cool the punch every few blows**, or it will upset or bend.
4. Drive to about **¾ of the thickness**. A **dark spot** appears on the underside: there the material has been chilled by the anvil, directly under the punch.
5. **Turn over** and punch through from the dark spot. Lay the bar over the pritchel hole, hardy hole or a bolster so the **slug** drops out.
6. **Drift** the hole open. The bar widens at the sides (eye). If necessary, forge the bulge down with the drift in place.
7. Punching distorts the bar (it gets shorter and bent). Straighten it on the face before removing the drift.

> [!tip] From the anvilfire iForge series
> Jock Dempsey (anvilfire.com, *Punching Holes*) advises punching out just the slug and not driving the punch too deep. A can of water under the hardy hole catches the punch and cools it at the same time.

## Steps (slit & drift)
1. Set the slit length by the rule of thumb (½ circumference of the hole, a bit shorter, since the slit stretches when drifting).
2. As with punching, drive the **slitter** in from both sides. Only when a thin web remains, cut through over the bolster. No slug (or a very thin one) falls out.
3. Open with **drifts of increasing size**, reheating in between. **Never** drive a drift fast in cold steel.

## Common faults
| Fault | Cause | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Hole off-centre | Punch tilted, inaccurate mark | Scribe a centre line, check after the first blow, lean the punch back if needed. |
| Punch stuck | No release agent, punch too hot | Coal dust, cool the punch, free it with light side blows. |
| Crack at the edge of the hole | Drifted too cold, hole too big for the section | Work hotter, slit instead of punching, upset first. |
