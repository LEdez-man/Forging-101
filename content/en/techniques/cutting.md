**Hot cutting** separates a piece in the heat, either on the **hardy** in the hardy hole or with a **hot cut chisel**. Cold cutting with a cold chisel only makes sense on thin, unhardened stock.

![[assets/diagrams/abschroten-en.svg|Hot cutting on the hardy]]

## Tools
| Tool | Edge angle | Use |
|---|---|---|
| Hardy | approx. 30–40° | in the hardy hole, work laid on top |
| Hot cut (handled chisel) | approx. 30° | on a cutting plate, with a striker or alone |
| Cold chisel | approx. 60° | cold, unhardened stock |

## Steps
1. Mark the cut. Bring the bar to **yellow heat**.
2. Lay it on the hardy and nick it with **medium blows**. Turn ¼ after each blow (square) or roll it (round).
3. Nick all round to about **⅓ remaining web**. **Never strike through onto the edge**: the hammer would hit the hardened edge (chipping, injury).
4. **Snap** the web over the anvil edge or part it with a light blow. The offcut flies: mind the direction, nobody in front of the anvil!
5. Forge or file off the burr if necessary.

> [!safety] Safety
> Offcuts are still very hot after a minute, even when they look cold. Put them in a bucket at once, not on the floor.
