**Splitting** divides a bar lengthwise with a **hot chisel**. You open the legs and forge each one on its own to make forks, split crosses, hearts, decorative ends, trivets and tong blanks. Along with [[en/techniques/punching|punching]] and [[en/techniques/cutting|hot cutting]], it is one of the smith's separating operations.

![[assets/diagrams/spalten-en.svg|Splitting – chisel, root of the split, opening]]

## Tools
- **Hot chisel / splitting chisel** with a handle, edge angle approx. 30°, made of tool steel
- **Cutting plate**: soft steel, copper or brass plate. **Never split on the anvil face**: it damages both face and edge.
- optional: chisel with rounded corner, drill for a relief hole

## Steps
1. Mark the length of the split. Secure the end with a centre-punch mark or a **3–5 mm drilled hole** if the root must be cleanly round.
2. Bring to **yellow heat**, lay on the cutting plate, set the chisel exactly **on the centre line** and drive it in to about half the thickness.
3. Turn over. The dark line from the other side shows the position. Cut from that side to the middle. Strike lightly just before breaking through.
4. **Cool the chisel often**. A hot chisel loses its hardness.
5. **Open** the legs with a chisel or over the anvil edge, keeping the root round.
6. The rough **split faces** are sharp-edged and often have a burr. Forge them down on the face ("break the edges") before shaping the legs further.

## Common faults
| Fault | Cause | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Crack runs beyond the root | Sharp root, opened too cold | Drill or round the root, open hot. |
| Legs of unequal size | Chisel off-centre | Scribe a centre line, check from both sides. |
| Chisel blunt / drawn temper | Cut on the face, not cooled | Use a cutting plate, cool, re-harden (→ [[en/advanced/heat-treatment\|Heat treatment]]). |
