**Drawing out** reduces the cross-section of a bar and makes it longer. It is the most common operation of all. Tapering, spreading and drawing tong reins are all variations of it.

![[assets/diagrams/strecken-en.svg|Drawing out – diagram]]

## Principle
Under the hammer, steel flows in the direction of least resistance. The contact area decides where it goes:
- **Broad contact** (flat hammer face on flat anvil face): material moves in all directions, i.e. length *and* width.
- **Narrow contact** (horn, cross-peen, fuller): material moves mainly at right angles to the narrow edge, i.e. lengthwise. So you rough out on the horn or with the peen, then smooth with the face (*planishing*).

The volume stays practically constant (→ [[en/basics/volume-constancy|Volume constancy]]).

## Tools
Forging hammer 1–1.5 kg (2–3 lb) with a slightly crowned face and radiused edges, anvil face, horn. Optional: cross-peen hammer, fuller or spring fuller (see [[en/techniques/fullering|Fullering]]).

## Steps
1. **Heat** to bright red to yellow (mild steel approx. 1050–1150 °C, see [[en/basics/heat-colours|Heat colours]]). Heat only as much length as you can work in one heat.
2. **Keep it square.** Two or three blows, turn 90°, two or three blows. Blows overlap and travel evenly along the section.
3. **Rough draw on the horn** or with the peen across the bar. This is faster but leaves ripples.
4. **Planish with the face.** Flatten the ripples, correct size and straightness.
5. **To go round:** forge to final size in square first, then knock off the corners (octagon), then 16 sides, then round. Round with light, quick blows while rotating constantly.
6. **Stop** at dark red (~750 °C) at the latest. Colder work creates stress and cracks and hurts your arm.

## Common faults
| Fault | Cause | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Section turns into a **rhombus** (diamond) | Section forged too flat (> 1 : 1.5) and then turned; uneven blows | Lay the bar with the **long diagonal vertical**, shorten it with light blows, then square up with a slightly tilted hammer. |
| **Cold shut** | Material pushed over a sharp edge (hammer edge, rhombus corner) | Grind out or cut off. Prevent by radiusing hammer edges and not letting the section tilt. |
| **Fish mouth** (hollow end) | Surface moves faster than the core, typical of too cold work or too light blows | Cut off the end. Work at full heat with heavier blows. |
| Half-moon hammer marks | Hammer lands tilted | Keep the face parallel to the anvil, check stance and anvil height (→ [[en/basics/hammer-control\|Hammer control]]). |
| Bent bar ("banana") | Struck harder on one side | Straighten on edge on the face, turn evenly. |

## Exercise
Fire poker: draw 12 mm (½") square to about 6 mm and round it, point, handle loop, optional twist. Black Bear Forge uses exactly this kind of project in his drawing-out lesson.
