**Upsetting** is the opposite of drawing out: the bar gets shorter and thicker where it is upset. You need it for heads (rivets, nails, bolts), collars, extra material before punching or forge welding, and for **sharp inside and outside corners** (upset square corner).

![[assets/diagrams/stauchen-en.svg|Upsetting – heat locally, upset in short bursts, straighten at once]]

## Principle
The blow acts along the bar axis. Only the hot, soft part deforms. **The heat decides where the upset happens.** Colder neighbouring zones act as abutments.

**Buckling rule:** keep the free, hot length to no more than about **2–3 times the diameter or width**. Otherwise the bar buckles instead of thickening. This rule of thumb comes from industrial heading.

## Methods
- **Upright on the face:** stand short bars on end, hot end up (or down), and strike the end.
- **Against the anvil side / upsetting block:** ram long bars horizontally against the side of the anvil or a block on the floor. The heavy workpiece then acts as its own hammer.
- **In the vise** with a heading tool (rivet head, nail header), see [[en/advanced/joinery|Joinery]].
- **Mid-bar:** heat a narrow zone, quench the neighbours, upset from both ends.

## Steps
1. Bring only the zone that should thicken to **yellow to white-yellow heat**. Cool the neighbouring zones with a ladle of water if needed.
2. Strike heavy, **straight** blows along the axis.
3. Check after a few blows. If the bar starts to bend, **straighten it on the face immediately**. A kink grows with every further blow.
4. Forge down the flanks of the bulge ("mushroom") as you go so no lap forms.
5. Work in several short heats rather than one long one.

## Common faults
| Fault | Cause | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Bar buckles | Hot zone too long, blows off-axis | Heat a shorter zone, straighten at once. |
| Mushroom with a lap | Rim spreads over and folds | Forge the rim down on the flanks in time. If a fold still forms, cut it off. |
| Upset in the wrong place | Heat not confined | Quench the neighbours, heat more precisely. |

## Application: upset square corner
A bent corner has a radius inside and too little material outside. For a sharp outside corner, upset the bend zone first, bend, then forge the corner out over the anvil edge with angled blows "from outside in". This is a classic test piece in the CBA/ABANA curriculum (Mark Aspery, *Upset Corners*).
