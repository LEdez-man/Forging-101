**Shouldering** (setting down) creates a defined **step in the section with a shoulder**. You need it for tenons, tangs, tong jaws, leaf stems, hook shanks and offsets. Unlike [[en/techniques/tapering|tapering]], the transition is *sharp* and in a specific place.

![[assets/diagrams/absetzen-en.svg|Shouldering – half-faced blow over the anvil edge]]

## Principle
The shoulder forms where the **anvil edge** supports the bar from below. The hammer strikes with a **half-faced blow**: half of the face is over the bar, the other half over or beyond the edge. Only the overhanging part is forged down.

- **One-sided:** bar on the edge, the shoulder forms underneath. With the half blow from above you also get an upper step.
- **Two-sided / all round:** turn over (180° or 90°) and **set down at exactly the same spot**. Aids: chalk mark, centre-punch mark, shoulder gauge.
- **With a set hammer** or **fuller**: for larger sections or with a striker. The set hammer places the shoulder precisely.
- **Guillotine tool** (spring fuller in a frame): sets the shoulder to size. Widely used by Black Bear Forge and in the CBA curriculum.

## Steps
1. Mark the shoulder position (chalk/centre punch).
2. Bring the bar to **yellow heat** and lay it over the **near anvil edge** with the mark exactly over the edge.
3. Strike half-faced blows with the hammer face **level**. A tilted hammer gives a sloping instead of a square shoulder.
4. Once set down, **draw out** the reduced part as usual.
5. For a crisp, square shoulder (tenon): finally reset the corner in the heat with the set hammer or on the edge.

> [!warning] Notch effect
> A sharp anvil edge leaves a **notch** that can crack under load. A slightly radiused edge (r ≈ 3–6 mm) leaves a fillet instead. That is why good anvils have edge sections with different radii.

## Related form: offset
An **offset** does not thin the bar but shifts it sideways in parallel. You make it with half-faced blows over the edge in both planes, see CCI *How to Forge Offsets and Bosses*. Typical in tong making.
