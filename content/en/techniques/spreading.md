**Spreading** makes a piece wider and thinner. It is drawing out with the flow direction turned through 90°. Uses include spatulas, spoons, leaves, screwdriver tips, hinges and fishtail ends.

![[assets/diagrams/breiten-en.svg|Spreading – the peen direction steers material flow]]

## Principle
The **cross-peen** acts like a narrow tool. Material flows **at right angles to the peen edge**.
- Peen **parallel** to the bar axis → material flows sideways → **spreading**
- Peen **across** the bar axis → material flows lengthwise → **drawing out**

You can also spread with the face alone, more slowly but more evenly, by fanning the blows out from the centre.

## Steps
1. If needed, first **isolate** the zone (see [[en/techniques/shouldering|Shouldering]]) so it is clear where the wider part starts.
2. Bring to **yellow heat**, lay flat on the face.
3. Work with the **peen parallel** to the bar in a row of overlapping blows from the centre outward. Turn over and repeat from the other side. This keeps the section symmetrical and stops the piece from curling.
4. **Planish** the ripples with the face.
5. **Dress the edges** on edge now and then. Thin edges cool fast and crack.
6. For spoon bowls or leaf curvature: dish into a hollow (swage block, wooden stump), see [[en/advanced/leaves-and-repousse|Leaves & repoussé]].

## Common faults
| Fault | Cause | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Edge cracks | Edges too cold / too thin | Reheat earlier, upset the edges on edge. |
| Piece curves sideways | Worked from one side only | Alternate sides, straighten on edge. |
| Uneven thickness | Blows not overlapping | Work in systematic rows, planish with the face. |
