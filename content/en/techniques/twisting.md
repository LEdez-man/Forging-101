**Twisting** rotates a bar about its long axis. On square and flat stock it produces a helical pattern of edges; round stock shows no visible twist. Twists serve as ornament, as grip texture and to orient ends (e.g. an eye turned through 90°).

![[assets/diagrams/tordieren-en.svg|Twisting in the vise with a twisting wrench]]

## Tools
- **Vise**, preferably a **leg vise** that can take blows
- **Twisting wrench**, or improvised: pipe wrench, open-end spanner, a second vise
- Chalk, water ladle

## Steps
1. Mark the twist zone (chalk or centre punch) and decide the number of turns.
2. Heat **evenly** to bright red–yellow. Even heat gives an even pitch. Uneven heat twists first where it is hottest.
3. Limit the zone by cooling the ends outside it with water.
4. Clamp the bar in the vise, set the wrench **at the zone boundary** and turn evenly. Do **not** bend the bar while twisting: use both hands on the wrench or support long bars.
5. Count the turns (chalk mark on one side of the wrench).
6. **Straighten:** the bar gets shorter and slightly bent. Straighten on a **wooden block with a wooden mallet** so the edges stay crisp.

## Variations
| Variation | Description |
|---|---|
| Simple twist | square bar twisted evenly |
| Reverse twist | two zones in opposite directions |
| Basket twist | 4 (or more) thin bars welded or riveted at the ends, twisted and then upset → basket |
| Pineapple twist | basket with a second, opposite twist |
| Rubik's cube twist | square divided into cubes with fullers, twisted alternately |
| Swaged twist | edges profiled in a swage before twisting |
