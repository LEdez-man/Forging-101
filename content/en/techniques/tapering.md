**Tapering** is drawing out with a steadily decreasing section toward the end. It is the starting point for hooks, scrolls, nails, punches, centre punches, chisels and almost every decorative end.

![[assets/diagrams/spitzen-en.svg|Tapering – working angle and the five taper sections]]

## The five cross-sections
In the CBA Level I curriculum Mark Aspery has students forge five tapers on one bar: **square, octagon, round, chisel (flat) and diamond**. The exercise trains hammer angle, turning rhythm and eye all at once.

## Steps
1. Bring the end to **yellow heat**. The very tip overheats fastest and can burn (sparking = burning).
2. **Raise the bar to the taper angle** (lower or raise your tong hand) and tilt the hammer to match. The tip lies at the edge of the face or slightly over the far edge.
3. **Forge it short and stout first** (a blunt pyramid), then draw it out. If you start long and thin straight away, you get a thin, cold tip you can no longer shape.
4. **Turn 90°** after every pair of blows to keep it square.
5. For round: square → octagon → round, as in [[en/techniques/drawing-out|drawing out]].
6. **Finish the tip last** with light blows in a falling heat. It cools fastest.

## Common faults
| Fault | Cause | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Hollow ("fish mouth") or split tip | Struck too cold, surface runs ahead of the core | Cut off and start again with more heat. |
| Tip rhombic / twisted | Uneven turning | Shorten the long diagonal, re-square. |
| Step instead of an even taper | Hammer too flat, blows not overlapping | Match the hammer angle to the taper angle. |
| Burnt tip (sparks, crumbly) | Overheated | Cut off. Burnt steel cannot be saved. |
