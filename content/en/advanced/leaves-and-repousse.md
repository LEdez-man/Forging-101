Leaves and flowers are the classic vocabulary of decorative ironwork. Technically they combine [[en/techniques/shouldering|shouldering]], [[en/techniques/drawing-out|drawing out]], [[en/techniques/spreading|spreading]] and repoussé.

## Simple leaf (sequence)
1. **Isolate** the leaf mass at the end of the bar from the stem with half-faced blows or a fuller.
2. **Draw out the stem**, forge the leaf mass into a diamond (lanceolate leaf) or a round disc (heart leaf).
3. **Spread** with the peen from the centre outward. Keep the margins thinner than the midrib.
4. **Vein** it hot with a veining chisel (blunt, rounded edge) on a soft support.
5. **Shape:** curve and twist it over the horn, in hollows or with tongs so the leaf looks alive.

## Repoussé
In **repoussé** you shape sheet or thin flat bar, hot or cold, from the back over stakes, in wooden hollows or on pitch, and refine it from the front with chasing tools. A showpiece is the **acanthus leaf**, the test piece of CBA Level III ("Repoussé – stake").

## Roses & flowers
Cut petals individually from sheet, dish them and rivet or weld them onto a tenon. Mark Aspery's three-part series *The Forged Rose* is among his most-watched videos.
