**Forge welding** is the oldest welding process (pressure welding, ISO 4063 process 43). Two pieces are brought to welding heat and fused by hammer blows. Uses: chain links, rings, tongs with welded-on reins, basket twists, inserted bits (axes), pattern welding and repairs.

![[assets/diagrams/feuerschweissen-en.svg|Lap weld: upset, scarf, lay on, forge out]]

## Prerequisites
1. **Clean surfaces:** no scale, no dirt. Scale prevents bonding.
2. **Welding heat:** approx. 1250–1350 °C for mild steel (white-yellow, the surface "sweats", first single sparks). Carbon steels weld somewhat lower.
3. **Reducing fire:** deep, clean heart of fire (coke), work in the fire, not in the air blast. Gas forge: slightly rich.
4. **Flux** (anhydrous borax, optionally with iron filings) sprinkled on from red heat. It dissolves scale and protects against fresh oxidation.

## Weld types
| Type | Description | Use |
|---|---|---|
| **Scarf / lap weld** | Both ends upset and scarfed, laid over each other | Extending a bar, the standard case |
| **Ring weld** | Ends of a bent ring scarfed | Rings, chain links |
| **Cleft weld** | One piece split, the other wedge-shaped and inserted | Heavy sections, inserted bits |
| **Butt weld** | End faces crowned and upset against each other | Heavy round bar |
| **Faggot weld** | Several bars bundled | Pattern welding, basket twists, using up offcuts |

## Sequence (lap weld)
1. **Upset:** upset both ends, since welding and scarfing cost section.
2. **Scarf:** forge a bevel of about 1.5 × thickness, **slightly convex**, so the first blow pushes scale and flux from the middle outward. The scarfs face each other when laid together.
3. Apply **flux** from red heat and bring slowly to welding heat, turning now and then.
4. **Lay together:** fast, without hesitating. Have the pieces ready on the face; the other hand or a helper places the second piece.
5. **First blows light and in the middle**, then toward the edges. Don't overwork: when the colour drops below bright red, take another welding heat.
6. Forge back to the original section and inspect the seam (a dark line = unwelded).

> [!safety] Safety
> Molten scale and borax spatter. Safety glasses, closed clothing and no spectators in front of the anvil. **Never forge-weld galvanized or stainless stock.**

## Mark Aspery's scarf theory
Aspery stresses that the **scarf angle** and **crown** decide success: too steep leaves notches at the end of the seam, too shallow leaves a thin edge that burns. His videos accompany an article in ABANA's *Hammer's Blow* (Winter 2011).
