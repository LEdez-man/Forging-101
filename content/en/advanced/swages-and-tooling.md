As soon as shapes repeat or sections must be precise, **forming tools** come in. They sit between open-die and closed-die forging.

## Hand tools
| Tool | Function |
|---|---|
| **Top/bottom swage** | Rounds, hexes or profiles a section (bottom swage in the hardy hole + handled top swage) |
| **Spring swage** | Top and bottom joined by a spring, for working alone |
| **Fuller** | Grooves, necks (→ [[en/techniques/fullering\|Fullering]]) |
| **Set hammer / flatter** | Shoulders, flat surfaces |
| **Swage block** | Cast block with hollows and holes of various shapes |
| **Guillotine tool** | Frame with interchangeable inserts (fuller, shoulder, cut-off), very precise |
| **Ball swage / rivet set** | Ball and rivet heads |

**Material:** swages for hot work from 1045 or 4140 (only lightly hardened or quenched and tempered), because faces that stay hot lose hardness anyway. Power-hammer dies from hot-work tool steel (e.g. 1.2714 / L6-type).

## Machines
- **Treadle hammer:** foot-operated, ideal for working alone with tooling
- **Power hammer** (mechanical/air): drawing heavy sections, pattern welding, repeat work. Christ Centered Ironworks sells plans for a home-built hammer.
- **Hydraulic forging press:** slower, deep material flow, popular for pattern welding
