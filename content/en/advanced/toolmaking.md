Smiths have always made their own tools. Toolmaking is also the best practice there is, because it combines every basic technique with accuracy and [[en/advanced/heat-treatment|heat treatment]].

## Tongs
**Sequence (flat tongs from square bar, "tongs without tongs"):**
1. Use a long bar so the cold end acts as a handle; you don't need tongs to make your first tongs (Torbjörn Åhman, Black Bear Forge).
2. **Set down the jaw**: half-faced blow over the edge, turn 90°, third step → jaw, boss (pivot area), start of the rein (→ [[en/techniques/shouldering|Shouldering]], [[en/techniques/fullering|Fullering]]).
3. **Draw out** the reins ([[en/techniques/drawing-out|Drawing out]]) or weld them on ([[en/advanced/forge-welding|Forge welding]], Mark Aspery).
4. Make two identical halves, [[en/techniques/punching|punch]] the pivot hole and **hot rivet** (→ [[en/advanced/joinery|Joinery]]).
5. **Fit them hot** to the work: warm the tongs, close them on bar of the right size, adjust.

## Punches, drifts, chisels
Forge them from 1045, 5160/51CrV4 (vehicle spring) or jackhammer bits (usually shock-resistant steels). Harden and temper the working end, leave the head soft and chamfer it to prevent mushrooming ([Black Bear Forge – Dressing hammers and punches](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Q5wyhqKtnqo)).

## Hammers
1. Cut a blank from 1045 (or O2/1.2842 for forging hammers).
2. **Punch and drift the eye**: oval or slightly hourglass-shaped so the wedge holds (→ [[en/techniques/punching|Punching]]).
3. Forge the **cheeks**, shape the peen and face.
4. **Differentially harden** face and peen, leave the eye soft.

Torbjörn Åhman's *Forging a Hammer by Hand* shows this whole sequence without machines, with striker work and home-made hammer tools.

## Axes
Two basic constructions:
- **Wrapped eye:** flat bar folded around a mandrel, ends welded, often with an inserted bit (historically typical).
- **Punched/drifted eye:** solid block, eye slit and drifted.

Nils Ögren specializes in axes: inserted bits ("hamburger technique"), pattern-welded edges, historical forms. For beginners: *Beginner's Guide to Forging Axes*.
