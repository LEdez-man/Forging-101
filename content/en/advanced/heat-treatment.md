Tools made of hardenable steel (chisels, punches, hammers, axes, knives) must be **heat treated** after forging. The theory comes from materials science (iron–carbon diagram, martensite formation). This page covers practical shop procedure.

## The steps
| Step | Purpose | Shop practice |
|---|---|---|
| **Normalizing** | Fine, uniform grain after forging | Heat 2–3 times to just above hardening temperature (non-magnetic + a little), cool in still air |
| **Annealing** | Make machining easier | To hardening temperature, then very slowly (in ash, vermiculite or the cooling forge) |
| **Hardening** | Martensite, maximum hardness | Heat evenly to hardening temperature (magnet test + approx. 30–50 °C, per data sheet), then **quench** in water, brine or oil (depending on the steel!) |
| **Tempering** | Reduce brittleness, set toughness | Straight after hardening, to tempering temperature (temper colour or kitchen oven, 1–2 h) |

## Temper colours as a tool table (guide values)
| Colour | ~°C | Tool |
|---|---|---|
| Pale yellow / straw | 200–230 | Files, razors, gravers |
| Gold / brown | 240–260 | Knives, centre punches, cold chisel edge |
| Purple / violet | 260–280 | Axes, woodworking tools |
| Blue | 290–320 | Springs, screwdrivers, hot cuts |

→ Colour chart: [[en/basics/heat-colours|Heat colours & temper colours]]

## Differential hardening
On chisels and punches only the **working end** is hardened. The shank stays soft, or the head will chip under the hammer. The classic method: quench the tip 2–3 cm, polish it bright and let the residual heat from the shank run to the tip until the desired temper colour arrives there. Then quench completely.

## Quench media
- **Water / brine:** very fast, risk of cracking; only for water-hardening steels (small 1045 sections, 1095)
- **Oil** (commercial quench oil, canola warmed to ~50 °C): for oil-hardening steels (5160/51CrV4, O1, O2)
- **Air:** for air-hardening steels (e.g. D2, some hot-work steels in small sections)

> [!warning] The data sheet beats the rule of thumb
> Hardening and tempering temperatures are steel-specific. Test-harden unknown steels first, spark-test them and check with a file.
