Traditional ironwork needs no arc welding. It is joined by **form** (tenon, pass-through), **friction/clamping** (collar, hot rivet) or **fusion** ([[en/advanced/forge-welding|forge welding]]). Good forged joints are design elements at the same time.

## Hot riveting
1. [[en/techniques/punching|Punch]] or drill the hole. Prepare the rivet (round head, countersunk) from mild steel or rivet stock.
2. **Rivet length** = grip length + about **1.5 × rivet diameter** for a round head (about 0.8–1 × d for countersunk).
3. Heat the rivet to yellow, insert it, back up the head in a **rivet set / dolly**, upset the tail first, then shape it with the set.
4. As it cools the rivet shrinks and pulls the parts together.

**Blind / tenon rivets:** a set-down tenon replaces the rivet and is peened over directly (see Black Bear Forge, *Blind riveting techniques*, *Plug welded rivets*).

## Hot collars
A **collar** wraps two or more bars and is applied hot; it shrinks as it cools and clamps.
1. Cut the collar stock (flat or half-round). **Length ≈ bundle perimeter + about 1.5–2 × collar thickness.** Make a test piece in wire.
2. Pre-bend the blank into a U.
3. Clamp the bundle in the vise, apply the collar at yellow heat and close the ends with a set hammer or collar tool.

## Tenon & mortise
- **Tenon** ([[en/techniques/shouldering|set down]], round or square, e.g. with guillotine and shoulder gauge) through a **mortise**, head riveted over.
- **Pass-through:** a bar passes through a slit-and-drifted bar (→ [[en/techniques/punching|Slit & drift]]). This is a core technique of German decorative ironwork tradition and of the CBA Level II grille.
- **Threaded tenon:** forged tenon with thread (Mark Aspery, *The Threaded Tenon*).
